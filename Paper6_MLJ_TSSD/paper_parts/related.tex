\section{Related work and positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Shift taxonomies and offline identification.} Covariate, label and concept shift are standard categories \citep{quinonero2009,moreno2012}, and concept-drift surveys cover adaptation after drift \citep{gama2014,lu2019}. Black-box shift detection \citep[BBSD;][]{rabanser2019} applies two-sample tests to classifier outputs. Label-shift estimation and correction use EM \citep{saerens2002}, confusion-matrix inversion \citep{lipton2018}, unified moment views \citep{garg2020} and bias-corrected calibration \citep{alexandari2020}. Closest in spirit to typing is the work of \citet{roschewitz2025}, who identify prevalence, covariate and mixed shift in medical imaging from batches. Their method is batch-based and has no sequential error guarantee.

\paragraph{Sequential and anytime-valid monitoring.} Classical change detection (CUSUM, Page--Hinkley \citep{page1954}, Shiryaev--Roberts) and stream-mining detectors such as DDM \citep{gama2004ddm} and ADWIN \citep{bifet2007adwin} monitor a single error signal. Test martingales and e-processes \citep{ville1939,howard2020,vovk2021,ramdas2023,waudbysmith2024} support inference under optional stopping. E-detectors \citep{shin2024edetectors} turn them into nonparametric change detectors with ARL control; our SR mode is an instance. On the shift side, \citet{podkopaev2022} track the risk of a deployed model with labels and detect \emph{harmful} shifts; \citet{amoukou2024} remove the need for labels through an error estimator; and \citet{zhang2026ppr} combine sparse true labels with synthetic labels through prediction-powered inference. Sequential two-sample tests by betting detect input shift with kernel witnesses \citep{shekhar2023} or online classifiers \citep{jang2022}. Conformal test martingales detect departures from exchangeability \citep{vovk2021retrain}, and \citet{vovk2020concept} decomposes them into concept and label components. WATCH \citep{prinster2025watch} weights conformal martingales to tolerate benign covariate shift while flagging harmful shifts. \citet{bar2024poem} and M-FISHER \citep{khan2025mfisher} couple martingale detection with test-time adaptation, and \citet{schirmer2025} monitor risk during test-time adaptation. Finally, \citet{choi2026confirm} gives an anytime-valid \emph{confirmation} of a prespecified label-shift correction through a predictive likelihood ratio, a special case of the factors used in our posterior-validity witness.

\paragraph{Positioning.} Table~\ref{tab:position} compares 17 methods on six properties. To our knowledge, no existing monitor combines the following: (a) sparse random labels together with unlabelled inputs; (b) a single anytime-valid budget for the alarm, with both ``ever'' and ARL control; and (c) a \emph{typed} claim with its own guarantee under the alternatives of the other types. The posterior-validity witness provides (c) for the decision that matters most operationally, retraining versus prior correction. It does so by testing a composite null that contains every covariate shift and a label shift at an unknown time, using universal inference \citep{wasserman2020}. Unlike WATCH, it does not need a density-ratio estimate to tolerate covariate shift. Unlike risk tracking, it detects posterior changes that leave the error rate unchanged (for example, label permutations at constant accuracy are not prior-correctable). TSSD does \emph{not} attempt adaptation, and its covariate-versus-label distinction carries no guarantee.
